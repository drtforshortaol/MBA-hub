iNaturalist — Credibility & Use

Volunteer reference · Not a guest-facing guide

Quick answer

iNaturalist is a credible international community biodiversity observation platform, not merely a local club. Founded as a UC Berkeley student project, it was associated with the California Academy of Sciences and National Geographic Society and became an independent nonprofit in 2023. Its records can be useful to scientists, but individual identifications vary in reliability.

Research Grade does not guarantee expert verification. It means an observation has met defined platform data-quality and community-identification criteria. An unusual sighting does not alone establish a population trend, range shift, or species rarity.

How to use it in MBA Hub

Guide-first principle: keep visitor-facing content short, memorable and accessible. Include a species name, one or two relevant observations, a guest-friendly takeaway and—when available—a link to the specific original record. Do not automatically create a separate app or display large galleries.

Distinguish what MJ personally observed, what other iNaturalist contributors reported, and what scientific studies establish. Preserve uncertainty (for example, “possibly kelp pipefish”). Prefer links to original records; do not republish third-party photos without checking licenses. If the original observation URL is unavailable, say so rather than inventing one.

Background for curious guides

Who contributes?

Naturalists, wildlife enthusiasts, specialists, and professional researchers contribute observations and identifications. The platform itself is not a government regulator or a final authority on species taxonomy.

What are the observation categories?

Casual: does not meet all requirements for verifiable observations. Needs ID: eligible for community identification but without sufficient consensus. Research Grade: meets applicable data-quality criteria and community identification agreement. An observation can still be misidentified.

How is it used by science?

Some iNaturalist observations are shared with the Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF) and can contribute to biodiversity and distribution research. Scientific conclusions require suitable sampling, verification and context; a count of app observations is not necessarily a population count.

Scientific source hierarchy

Use appropriate peer-reviewed studies and specialist taxonomic resources for scientific conclusions and accepted names; institutional monitoring for population trends; iNaturalist for documented individual occurrence reports; and firsthand guide observations as dated local observations.
